# Battle System Design

## Purpose

This document records the game rules of the Battle component (`internal/core/battle`): legacy evidence, approved decisions and open gaps.

Battle is an independent, reusable domain component. It resolves combat between participants and determines outcomes without knowing why the battle was started (e.g., adventure quest, arena, dungeon, boss, or guild battle).

Domain contracts and formulas are owned by the code and tests under `internal/core/battle` and `internal/battle`; generated combat messages belong to `internal/api/http`.

Evidence paths are under `party2/lib/` at revision `ff97fc549932a146cc945d7edf71b44c868a840e` unless stated otherwise. Numeric item, gem and job references are provenance identifiers, not authorization to reuse distinctive display names.

## Batch resolver

The batch resolver (`ResolvePartyBattle`) is the automatic, agility-ordered resolver used by non-interactive combat modes. It is not the original command-by-command normal-adventure combat (`_battle.cgi`, `_skill.cgi`); see [Adventure](adventure.md#original-interaction-contract-and-reconstruction-target). Reconstruction reuses verified combat effects, but not the batch action selection, agility round order or automatic termination as the interaction rule.

Legacy rules the batch resolver preserves:

- Virulent poison (劇毒) deals 10% MaxHP (capped at 950–1049 when >999) to every other afflicted living party member on each ally action (`_battle.cgi:792–807`), then to the afflicted actor (`_battle.cgi:810`); no natural cure.
- Deadly poison (猛毒) deals the same damage to the actor at the end of its own turn (`_battle.cgi:810`); no natural cure.
- Poison (毒) deals the same damage at the end of the actor's turn and then cures at 1/5 if the actor is alive (`_battle.cgi:840`).

Known differences of the batch resolver, outside the explicit-action reconstruction:

- Revival uses `CheckRevival`'s ability-based approximation (fixed undying recovery, quarter-HP item193, trigger consumption). The explicit engine uses [the ordered revival contract](#resources-revival-and-roster-changes).
- The 100-iteration draw is a safeguard, not a legacy termination rule; an interactive encounter must not inherit it or any other fictional operation timeout.
- Cursed actors skip current/previous-job and custom skills without MP/CMP debit. Holding item249 applies the per-entry magician-name classification (`_battle.cgi:764–780,1804–1825`; `_skill.cgi:68–77,263–273`) rather than halving every magic-category skill.

Magic rules shared with the explicit engine (`_skill.cgi:8811–8823,9081–9135,9954–10144`):

- Magic attacks use direct base power without physical attack scaling and bypass defense. Item149 (×1.1 direct) skips the tension multiplier and keeps tension; item143 doubles (truncated) when power <300; a `kinju` target takes ×1.25; item144 scales area hits ×1.3 when modified power <300. Tension is consumed on the first effective hit. Successive area targets decay ×0.85 unless item144 is held with initial power ≤300.
- Magic healing applies item146 (×1.8), item256 (×1.2 plus living-ally splash capped at 30% of the primary target's MaxHP), caster `sabaku` (×0.7–0.9) and target virulent-poison truncation `int(v×0.1)` before variance and tension, then resets tension to 1 regardless of item149.
- A magic category seal cures at 25%; on failure MP is still debited and the effect aborts without consuming tension.

### Shared continuation rules

These rules apply to every callback family below; sections do not repeat them.

- **Whole-effect admission.** An action is available only when its whole reachable effect has an evidenced native implementation: callback-specific targets, resources, resets, death/revival, roster mutations and durable side effects. Learning, shop or synthesis availability does not certify a battle effect, and shared helper presence does not certify concrete entries. Unknown effects never become ordinary attacks; an empty custom action never spends CMP. The gate applies to player, helper and ordinary-enemy actions, including random or delegated invocation. Missing behavior remains a cutover prerequisite, never a permanent omission.
- **Preflight and execution.** The enclosing operation preflights every reachable continuation before cost, RNG or mutation. A missing descendant rejects the enclosing (including indirect) choice without narrowing its original draw pool. Exact writes project into later admission; after an unknown (random) transition, later slots are checked for registration only, and the executor validates each callback against the actual state immediately before it runs. A rejection there restores the caller's battle state and callback context, and the consumer commits nothing. **Decision (2026-11-17, #1733):** random outcomes cannot be projected exactly, and the source always executes these sequences, so execution-time validation replaces whole-sequence rejection. A supported operation with no eligible target is an accepted no-effect action and is charged normally.
- **Ordered tables.** Job tables keep every original position, including positions owned by other sections (wallet, inspection, field, book, revival, arithmetic). Registering one family never overwrites another family's binding.
- **Costs.** Listed ordinary costs belong to the invoking consumer and are charged after the callback, including accepted no-effect and caster death, unless the callback reports `$mes`. Random/tool MP restoration and delegated extended debits follow [random and delegated invocation](#random-and-delegated-job-closure).
- **Persistence.** All behaviorally relevant facts survive serialize/reload: ordered participants, resources, item instances/consumption, banishment, crystal drops, temporary/extended effects, tension/hit, CMP recharge, room skill bans, per-side history and temporary/default/anti fields. Persist encounter identities and randomized outcomes already produced; retries read committed results rather than rerolling. Save the frozen input and resulting facts with the consumer's receipt, rebuild runtime ports on reload, and return the saved receipt on retry without re-executing callbacks. Neither the RNG nor deferred executable work is saved.

### Operation boundaries

Normal-action temporary expiry preserves the player mirror-sharing/item163 exceptions and consumes the original random draw even for empty temporary state. Reflection/cover redirects persist; only one-hit defense is consumed at its damage-helper point. Reset restores tension1/hit95 and clears status/effects, then reapplies current equipment separately for requester, actor and target; a cached encounter-start profile cannot replace equipment callbacks. HP/MP/CMP, consumed items, bans and history survive reset. Explicit advance performs the first living item188 holder's ally revival pass, resets members with current equipment, reapplies living holders' revival temporary effect, and clears temporary/anti fields while retaining the default.

### Ordinary NPC and stage boss state

**NPC order decision:** preserve the original head rotation, including skipped heads. For enemy order `[A,B,C]`, when A is not interval-ready and B acts, the result is `[B,C,A]`, not `[A,C,B]`. Only B's action timestamp changes. With no eligible living NPC, no rotation occurs. Selection is `_npc_action.cgi:90–92`; rotation is `149–153`. Persist semantic order independently of SQL lock order.

Adventure's ordinary tables retain all 315 source template sites (74 boss, 241 monster), including branch order and weighted appearances. Stage15 copies each ordered ally's jobs/current stats and maximum resources, and generates ten monster slots with jobs 0–87 and previous job 36/SP999. Preserve the complete generated summon pool as well as selected enemies. Stage25 chooses one of three three-boss rosters; stage26 uses room-marker priority spring/summer/autumn/winter, defaulting to spring. Reload never selects a branch or rolls stats again. Ordinary stage23 has the job21/SP10/MP30 boss; job406 is EX-only (`stage/15.cgi:13–66`; `25.cgi:16–204`; `26.cgi:16–28,623–1218`; `23.cgi:15–55`). The floor-12 EX tables (`@ex_bosses`, stages 22–25 and each stage26 season) use the same template conventions, boss scaling and summon pool; colliding source names share the ordinary encounter identity.

Initialization rolls each current stat as floor(base×(0.9+rand(0.3)+(party size−2)×0.1)); supplied nonzero maxima win. Hard ordinary monsters scale SP, previous SP and rewards by 1.5 before stat rolls. Custom CMP cost uses occupied slots, including duplicates, with the 1/1.1/1.2 multiplier for one/two/three slots.

Ordinary stage24 floor10 applies the special party caps (HP/MP maxima 999, base stats 255, doubled for over-level actors), then shared equipment reset; it does not install an EX default field. Adventure owns [travel and EX progression](adventure.md#combat-travel-and-encounter-replacement), including tool150's all-member reset. Curse/specter hooks require a reachable producer; numeric ordinary stages supply none, and unsupported specter payloads remain gated.

### Dynamic effects and catalog admission

Definitions map catalog IDs to validated native payloads; learning or commerce definitions cannot create combat entries by matching kind strings. Occupied custom slots hydrate in original order, including duplicates, only when every slot passes; otherwise the whole custom choice is omitted. Discovery and submission derive from the same server-owned definitions; client-submitted payloads are never trusted.

Ordinary held tools keep the distinct tool helper category: HP and MP tools use their recovery helpers; damage tools supply fixed base power rather than adding actor attack. Omitted held-tool selectors default to self; ambient-cursor bodies use the request cursor. Temporary assignments keep each body's living guard, stat-helper return and order. Full reset reapplies equipment to every ordered member, including fallen members, before refilling maxima (`_data.cgi:623–1340,1527–1535,1641–1675,1879–1889`; `_skill.cgi:dougu:758–764`; `_battle.cgi:reset_status_all:1411–1431`).

Inspection callbacks reveal only the information selected by the body and never grant loot; the encounter owner freezes treasure predicates, reward definitions/kinds and prices. Tool213 compares strictly against frozen persistent requester MaxHP, not the temporary actor's MaxHP.

#### Random and delegated job closure

The draw is over **job tables and ordered callback positions**, not distinct names, learned skills or native definitions. The player table ends at job87 (`_data.cgi:12–301`). Job73 supplies a generated view of another table, not an independent catalog. Commented entries at `_skill.cgi:3241,3689` are excluded.

General random invocation draws each job1..87 with equal probability, then a position in that job's table. Extended positions are redrawn within the same table; repeated names remain separate positions, so callbacks in differently sized tables have different probabilities. No concrete table or job73 proxy is empty or all-extended at this revision. The helper has an accepted empty-table no-effect branch; an all-extended table would never finish selection, so a future such table needs an explicit decision, not a retry limit or supported-only pool. Tool84 has no empty-table guard and no extended-entry exclusion (`_data.cgi:1260–1264`).

#### Arithmetic selectors and digit reversal

Evidence: `_skill.cgi:2653–2713,5450–5581`. These callbacks check the acting participant's magic seal, then write directly to the ordered member list of both sides, bypassing target redirection, immunity, damage/healing modifiers, tension, darkness recoil and damage/risk/helper counters. Member order is captured at callback entry, while HP/MP eligibility is reread after each defeat hook. Job31 digit reversal alone takes a target: a supplied participant on either side is used directly, even when fallen; only an absent one falls back to the untyped ally choice. Negative two/three-character HP strings remain unsupported.

Job72's divisor wrappers parse the second `＠×…` command from the submitted text and resolve it against the complete discovered name map, where the last matching entry wins; positional random and first-match delegation never repeat this lookup. No match is an accepted failed cast. A discovered unsupported last-match child rejects the whole selector, even when an earlier duplicate is supported. The divisor is a request global set only by a wrapper with a matched child and never reset: nested callbacks and the NPC response of the same command (`_battle.cgi:1170`) continue from it, and it is never saved. An NPC's own wrapper never matches, because the NPC turn clears the submitted text (`_npc_action.cgi:95–97`). A direct effect entry without one still checks the magic state, then fails to cast.

#### Shared modifiers and callback outcomes

Evidence: `_skill.cgi:8807–9770,9948–10194,10384–10430,10559–10624,11035–11104`; `_battle.cgi:28–142,748–1178,1390–1428,1715–1759,1855–1873`. Callback guards, coefficients and tails remain part of each entry's support boundary.

**Categories.** Callback category (physical, magic, breath, dance, song or untyped) is independent of element and of direct/normal-attack flags. Category seals check only magic/dance/physical, each with a fresh 1/4 cure attempt. Enemy-side category conversion precedes that check: item166, eligible noncustom magic-sword commands, then seal12 with its 3 MP charge. The magic-sword multiplier and replacement of trailing arguments with direct1 apply to every category on that whitelist; only its physical-to-magic conversion is category-dependent (`10171–10184`), so item166's untyped result still receives the multiplier. An elemental multiplier cannot replace these rules. Status/death gem and projectile bodies convert symbolic payloads to zero arithmetically, including seal12's 3 MP charge before later helper gates.

**Targets and temporaries.** Resolve the target before applying its temporary effect once. Cover selects the first same-side covering member, without a living filter. Defense/reduction/double can modify numeric healing or buff coefficients as well as damage; symbolic payloads bypass numeric multipliers. Matching reflection redirects to the actor (item163 doubles nonzero numeric payloads), nullification sets zero, and deflection/phantom use their own ordered draws. #### Resources, revival and roster changes

HP recovery and MP recovery are separate operations. MP recovery (`_mp_h:9150–9187`) resolves a living ally and applies the shared recovery modifiers. After a nonzero result it clears rage, rounds random variation times actor tension, then adds 1. Target items189/202 exclude recovery; item198 excludes it only when the actor's MP is strictly above floor(actor MaxMP×0.5). These exclusions still consume tension. Recovery caps at MaxMP; magic in sanctuary subtracts overflow after the cap, with a floor of 1. Den applies floor(modified power×0.025)+1 after earlier zeroing.

MP reduction (`_st_down:9252–9290`): base defense above 999, item159 or curse prevents it; the floor is 20%MaxMP. The requested debit is floor(current MP×modified coefficient×actor tension). Only a debit strictly above 150×tension is replaced by floor(random[0,50)+100×tension). Return that requested debit even when the floor limits actual loss. MP absorption passes this amount into MP recovery; it never changes HP.

## Other combat rules

- **Agility exceed check (`_is_exceed_ag`).** Standard: 1/3 (`rand(3)==0`) and rand(actor AG) ≥ rand(target AG×3). Item147: 1/2 and rand(actor AG) ≥ rand(target AG). It is used for both the critical roll (actor vs target) and the evasion roll (target vs actor).
- **Cursed actors** cannot choose or execute custom or current/previous-job skills (`_skill.cgi:35–36,84–85,293`).
- **Weapon71** base attack is int(mat×(0.75+rand0.5)), or int(mat×(1.5+rand0.5)) with item158 or awakening gems 240–242 in the accessory slot; level factor 0.5 below 50, 0.7 below 75, otherwise 1.0 (`_battle.cgi:1736–1744`). Weapons69/70 add int(min(level,99)×1.5) / ×2.0 (`_battle.cgi:1728–1734`).
- **Awakening gem accessories** (`_data.cgi:1997–1999`): item240 +30 AT/−20 DF/−20 AG; item241 −20 AT/+30 DF/−20 AG; item242 −20 AT/−20 DF/+30 AG.
- **Monster kills** (`kill_m`): on victory, each surviving character counts every defeated non-player enemy that is strong relative to the player (`_battle.cgi:230–235`). The demon-king unseal counter is driven by the explicit [stage19 treasure event](adventure.md#combat-travel-and-encounter-replacement), not by victory; see [Boss unsealing](boss.md#6-unseal-to-sealing-room-lifecycle).
- **Damage floor scope.** The physical base formula has a 1-or-2 fallback. Misses, evasion, immunity and later modifiers can yield zero; it is not a minimum for every attack or skill.
- **Context isolation.** The battle engine never queries persistence or mutates character state; feature modules persist results through `internal/battle`.
